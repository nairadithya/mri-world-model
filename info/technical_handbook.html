What an MRI can tell us

An MRI is a measurement of tissue-dependent signal. It is not a photograph of tumor biology. That distinction governs every experiment in this handbook.

A glioma is a tumor arising from the brain's glial lineage. Following a treated glioma requires comparing examinations acquired at different visits. A bright region may reflect tumor, treatment-related change, fluid, or other processes. Image appearance alone does not settle which explanation is correct.

One examination, several kinds of contrast

Magnetic resonance imaging, abbreviated MRI, uses magnetic fields and radiofrequency excitation to measure signal related to tissue properties. A sequence is an acquisition recipe. Different recipes emphasize different properties. In this project, a visit may contain four structural sequences.

From an image to a measurement

A segmentation assigns image locations to labeled regions. A binary mask marks whether a location belongs to a region. The project measures three automated lesion compartments, meaning three labeled anatomical regions.

  • Necrotic/nonenhancing compartment. The target field is necrotic_non_enhancing. Its automated label is not independent confirmation of histological necrosis.
  • Enhancing compartment. The target field is enhancing.
  • Edema/FLAIR compartment. The target field is edema_flair. The name describes an imaging-label category, not a clean biological partition.

These masks are useful proxy targets, measurable stand-ins for a capability of interest. Predicting an automated mask well establishes agreement with that target. It does not, by itself, establish accurate disease measurement. If the automated target is wrong, a model can learn that error faithfully.

Clinical response is a different target

RANO, Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology, is a clinical response framework. The four clean project categories are progressive disease, stable disease, partial response, and complete response, abbreviated PD, SD, PR, and CR. Formal response assessment uses more than a single automated volume change, including clinical and treatment-related context. RANO 2.0 is background reading here, not evidence that this dataset's historical labels were adjudicated under that version.

A surgery-related rating, a missing label, and stable disease are different observations. They must not be merged into one convenient class. Likewise, treatment is an intervention, while a response label describes an outcome. A response label is not a treatment action.

The first distinction

The project asks whether useful information about anatomy and change survives in numerical image features. Automated lesion measurements are one way to test that question. They are neither the entire objective nor biological ground truth.

Project definitions come from Methods, labels and outcomes and the research guardrails. Clinical background is supported by Villanueva-Meyer and colleagues' imaging review and Wen and colleagues' RANO 2.0 criteria, listed in References. This is not a diagnostic handbook.

Before moving on. Be able to distinguish an MRI sequence, an automated mask, a physical volume, and a clinical response label. They describe different levels of evidence.

Why coordinates come before models

A mask can have the right shape in memory and still lie over the wrong part of the brain. Array compatibility is not anatomical compatibility.

A three-dimensional image is stored as an array of voxels, the volume equivalents of pixels. Array indices such as (i, j, k) locate a value in memory. They do not tell us where that value lies in physical space.

The affine is the map, not the territory

An affine is a matrix mapping voxel indices into a declared physical coordinate system. It encodes orientation, spacing, and position. NIfTI, a common medical-image format, stores image data and header information that can supply this mapping. The physical units must be inspected rather than assumed.

[x, y, z, 1]T = A [i, j, k, 1]TA maps an array location into a physical location. Matching array dimensions does not imply matching A.

If the spatial part of the affine is measured in millimeters, the physical volume of one voxel is the absolute determinant of its upper-left three-by-three matrix. For an orthogonal grid, this is the product of the three voxel spacings. Native mask volume is the number of included voxels multiplied by that voxel volume.

Registration, resampling, and resizing

Registration estimates a transformation that aligns images. Resampling evaluates an image on a new grid using a coordinate mapping and interpolation. Resizing changes array dimensions. These operations are related, but they are not interchangeable.

The BRAINIAC input contract includes correction for smoothly varying intensity bias, physical resampling, optional registration when a template is supplied, removal of nonbrain tissue, a final resize to 96 voxels on each axis, and intensity normalization. A 96-cubed input contains 884,736 locations. It does not follow that each of those locations represents one cubic millimeter.

Masks use nearest-neighbor interpolation when aligned to a grid so that interpolation does not invent fractional class labels. Images can use different interpolation rules. Independently resizing an image and a mask as arrays can discard the coordinate relationship between them. The repaired project pipeline aligns masks through verified physical grids before the final resize.

Several kinds of correctness

  1. File correctness. The array loads, and its values are finite.
  2. Geometric correctness. The declared physical map is valid, and image and mask grids are aligned.
  3. Measurement correctness. Native volume arithmetic and label mappings are consistent.
  4. Target validity. The mask accurately represents the intended anatomy, consistently across visits and cohorts.
  5. Biological interpretation. An observed difference corresponds to the disease process of interest.

Passing an earlier level does not establish the next. An affine does not prove registration accuracy. A plausible overlay does not establish repeatability. Repeatability means that repeated measurement of the same underlying state gives sufficiently similar results.

Missing is not empty

An absent MRI sequence requires an explicit availability indicator. A nonfinite value, often called NaN, is not a valid observation. A missing mask is not a measured zero-volume compartment. Conversely, a genuinely empty compartment is a real zero that should remain in the evaluation. Excluding difficult empty cases after seeing predictions would change the question being answered.

See the preprocessing and geometry contract, missing and zero-volume rules, and NiBabel's coordinate-system documentation. The calculation for 96 cubed is explanatory arithmetic, not an empirical finding.

Before moving on. Explain why correct native volume and incorrect lesion placement can coexist. The measurement of size and the placement of that measurement on the image are separate checks.

Three questions that must stay separate

Estimating what is present, measuring a difference we have already observed, and predicting a future observation are different tasks. A successful result on one cannot be relabeled as a success on another.

Let xt denote the MRI at visit t, vt its measured compartment volumes, and Ht all information available through that visit. An index such as t+1 means the next eligible observed visit. It need not represent a fixed number of days.

State and observed change

State estimation predicts a measurement from the current scan. Observed-change assessment uses two scans that are both available. A specialized pair model may compare the scans jointly, but a crucial simple control estimates each state separately and subtracts the estimates.

That subtraction control asks whether the paired architecture adds anything beyond competent single-scan measurement. Beating a predictor of no change is not enough to establish that specialized paired modeling is necessary.

Future prediction and its cutoff

A forecast uses only information available at a specified prediction cutoff. Its inference inputs cannot include the later scan, later mask, later volume, or a gap ending at the later visit when that gap is not known at the cutoff. Future observations can be training targets without being inference inputs. A training target tells the model what it should predict; an input tells it what it is allowed to know.

The project generally targets the next observed visit rather than an exact future day. A retrospectively recorded interval describes when the next visit happened. It is not necessarily a planned interval known to a clinician or model beforehand. Giving that interval to a model changes the task to a conditional diagnostic.

Persistence is a meaningful comparator

Persistence predicts that the future measurement equals the current measurement. For a change target, it predicts zero change. This is often difficult to beat when measurements change slowly, future events are uncertain, or measurement noise is substantial. A sophisticated architecture does not get credit merely for being more complicated.

Predicted volume change = 0These are equivalent persistence baselines for the corresponding targets. A mask-assisted current measurement and an MRI-only learned estimate must be labeled separately.

Nuisance and causality

Acquisition nuisance means variation not intended as the disease signal, such as intensity changes caused by an acquisition setting. A synthetic perturbation of one scan can test whether a model invents change when the anatomy was not deliberately altered. But blurring an image can destroy anatomical detail, so not every perturbation cleanly isolates nuisance.

The records sometimes call a future-excluding model causal. Here that means chronological input validity. It does not identify the causal effect of a treatment. Predicting an observed outcome is different from estimating what would have happened under an alternative treatment.

The distinctions are fixed in the assessment protocol, the forecast contract, and Notes and insights.

What a representation is, and how to test one

A representation is an intermediate numerical description of an image. The important question is not whether it exists, but which useful information it preserves.

An encoder is a function, commonly a neural network, that converts an image into numerical features. We write those features as z = f(x). A latent representation is another name for those internal features. A vector stores a list of numbers; a spatial feature map retains a grid of feature vectors.

A decoder maps features back to an output such as a segmentation. A head is an output component for a particular task. A readout extracts a quantity from features. A probe is a readout used to investigate what the representation makes accessible.

Learning, supervision, and loss

Neural networks contain adjustable parameters, also called weights. A loss assigns a numerical penalty to an incorrect training output. Gradient descent changes parameters in directions intended to reduce that loss. An optimizer implements the update rule. One epoch is one pass through the scheduled training data.

Supervision is the target information supplied during training. A single volume per compartment is scalar supervision. A mask supplies a target at many image locations and is called dense supervision. A model receiving dense masks has more task-specific supervision than one receiving only volumes. A comparison between them cannot attribute an improvement to architecture alone.

Mask supervision does not imply mask input at inference. A model can learn from masks during training and receive only MRI when used. This distinction is essential for the R35 and R36 studies discussed later.

Pretrained, adapted, and frozen

A pretrained encoder starts from weights learned before the current experiment. Fine-tuning adapts those weights to the new task. A frozen encoder keeps its weights unchanged while a readout is fitted. These are different fitting budgets, not interchangeable labels for one method.

LoRA, low-rank adaptation, learns a constrained update to a pretrained weight matrix while keeping the original matrix fixed. It reduces the number of trainable parameters. It does not guarantee good MRI features or a successful temporal model. The historical project adapted BRAINIAC, a pretrained MRI backbone, through LoRA.

Why readout capacity matters

A linear readout applies weighted sums to features. A nonlinear readout can combine features in more complicated ways. A multilayer perceptron, or MLP, is a feed-forward neural network and is one nonlinear option. Better predictions from a larger readout can result from better decoding rather than a better representation.

Ridge regression is a linear model penalized for large coefficients. Its regularization strength controls how strongly that penalty acts. This matters when feature vectors have many dimensions but the number of independent patients is small. Selecting that strength must use training data or internal validation, not the evaluation outcomes.

A narrow probe is not an oracle

Failure of a linear probe does not prove that information is absent from the encoder. It means the stated fitting recipe did not extract the intended gain. Conversely, a powerful decoder's success does not establish general-purpose encoder quality.

Terminology and fitting distinctions follow Methods. See the original LoRA paper for the adaptation mechanism. The project's use of that mechanism must be judged by its own experiments.

Predicting features without predicting pixels

The original model predicts a later scan's representation. This avoids reconstructing every image intensity, but it does not automatically produce useful disease dynamics.

The JEPA idea

A joint-embedding predictive architecture, abbreviated JEPA, learns to predict a target representation from a context representation. The original image-based I-JEPA paper predicts representations of target image regions from other regions. This repository adapts the general idea to longitudinal MRI visits. The image paper does not independently validate the temporal medical adaptation.

The historical pipeline encodes available MRI sequences with BRAINIAC, combines their features with clinical information under the stated configuration, summarizes the observed history, and predicts the next representation. A transformer is a neural architecture that combines information across tokens through attention. A token is one vector presented to that architecture. Here, visit tokens summarize a patient's sequence of observations.

The slowly moving teacher

An exponential moving average, or EMA, updates a target network as a weighted average of its old weights and the current online weights. The online network receives ordinary training updates; the target network changes more slowly. A projector maps encoder features into the space used by the loss.

Target weights ← m × old target weights + (1 − m) × online weightsThe momentum m is close to one. This is a training mechanism, not an uncertainty estimate or a guarantee of meaningful features.

The historical loss compares normalized vectors using cosine error. Cosine similarity measures how similarly two vectors point, independent of their lengths; cosine error is one minus that similarity. A low cosine error does not imply that a clinically important volume, boundary, or treatment effect is predicted accurately.

Collapse and implementation checks

Collapse occurs when different inputs produce nearly identical features. A collapsed representation may make a training objective easy while discarding the differences we care about. Feature standard deviation measures variation. Effective rank summarizes how many directions of variation are represented. Healthy values are useful diagnostics, not downstream success criteria.

Automatic mixed precision, or AMP, uses lower-precision arithmetic for parts of training. It saves memory but can overflow in large reductions. A gradient describes how a loss changes with a parameter or input. Checking gradient flow can verify that intended components receive a learning signal and future inputs do not influence a prediction. Gradient accumulation adds gradients from several passes before an optimizer update. It changes the effective update batch without requiring all images to be held in memory at once.

Time encoding is part of the model contract

Giving each visit its previous gap is not the same as giving it elapsed time from the start of the history. Equal-gap tokens can leave chronological order ambiguous. R34 introduced a separately declared elapsed-time option and checkpoint compatibility checks. Matching saved tensor shapes does not establish matching time semantics.

The default JEPA loss was also found to update the backbone's LoRA parameters and predictor, but not the online projector. That is a documented property of this implementation. It is not a general rule for every JEPA, and repairing or changing it would be a separate experiment.

See chronology and projection semantics, the model implementation, and the original I-JEPA paper. The handbook does not infer useful forecasting from healthy monitors or finite gradients.

Who was used to fit what?

A performance number has no clear meaning until we know which patients trained the encoder, selected the model, fitted the readout, and supplied the evaluation observations.

A cohort is a group of participants used in a study. Training data fit parameters. Validation data select settings or checkpoints. Evaluation data measure performance under a declared protocol. A checkpoint is a saved set of model parameters. If evaluation results repeatedly influence choices, that cohort becomes development evidence even if its directory is still called test.

The patient is not the visit

One patient contributes several correlated scans. Randomly splitting visits can place one person's earlier scan in training and later scan in evaluation. For a claim about unseen patients, all visits of a person must stay in the same group. Cross-validation, or CV, rotates which patient groups are held out. It does not create new independent patients.

A nested evaluation uses an inner training-only procedure to select readout settings and an outer procedure to evaluate them. It prevents setting selection from directly using the outer outcomes. It still cannot undo earlier development on the same cohort.

The fitting budget belongs beside the result

A fitting budget includes the number and identity of source patients, labels, training epochs, feature dimensions, tuning choices, and model-selection opportunities. Comparing a densely supervised model with a frozen scalar readout is not an architecture-only comparison. Fitting a readout within the assessment cohort by CV is also not the same protocol as fitting it entirely on separate source patients.

The later studies use the name source52 for a readout trained on 52 source patients. Source26 refers to a separate 26-patient readout budget under its own protocol. Neither should be confused with the 26 encoder-unseen assessment patients. Equal counts do not establish equal participants or equal experimental roles.

Source-only transfer

Transfer evaluates a model or readout on a different cohort or acquisition setting. Zero-shot transfer uses no fitting on target-cohort outcomes. Support-set adaptation fits using a declared subset of target participants and evaluates on different participants. Transductive procedures use information from the evaluation collection itself. These regimes answer different questions.

A normalization step or regularization choice can leak outcome information even if the encoder never changes. A normalizer rescales features or targets using fitted statistics. Source-only normalization estimates those statistics without evaluation outcomes. Selecting the best preprocessing or readout after looking at transfer results also spends evaluation independence.

Two different kinds of generalization

Performance on a new patient from a familiar development setting differs from performance at a genuinely independent site. If a second cohort has been repeatedly examined during development, its results are informative transfer evidence, not untouched external validation. Data access, label definitions, and patient overlap must be checked before stronger claims.

A clean split is necessary, not sufficient

Encoder-unseen means the stated encoder was not trained on those patients. It does not mean the researchers have never examined their outcomes. A renamed test set does not recover independence.

See cohort identity rules and the historical locked RANO protocol. The grouped cross-validation documentation explains why groups must remain intact.

Read the unit before the score

A small number can be a poor result, and a larger number can be a good one. The target, baseline, unit, and averaging rule decide what the score means.

Volumes and their logarithms

Mean absolute error, or MAE, averages the absolute difference between prediction and target. Lower is better. Raw volume error measured in cubic millimeters emphasizes large lesions. The project commonly uses the natural logarithm of one plus the numerical volume in mm³, written log1p(v). The added one keeps a genuinely empty compartment finite.

st = log(1 + vt)
Δs = st+1 − stv is the numerical native volume in mm³. A log-volume change is not a raw-mm³ change, a clinical response category, or an exact physical growth rate.

A log-volume MAE has log-volume units, not mm³. It compresses large numerical volumes and can make errors near a measured zero particularly consequential. Because log1p includes an additive one, it is not invariant to changing the unit used to express volume.

Local occupancy is a different outcome

Occupancy records whether a voxel belongs to a compartment. A signed occupancy difference is later mask minus earlier mask. Values of +1 mark appearance, −1 mark disappearance, and 0 mark unchanged occupancy. It is not a difference between MRI intensities.

A whole-grid loss averages errors over all voxels. If changed voxels are scarce, unchanged locations dominate it. A balanced change error first averages within the changed and unchanged groups, then gives the nonempty group means equal weight. If there are no changed voxels, only the unchanged group contributes. It is a declared research endpoint, not a clinical scale.

A zero-change predictor can have deceptively low whole-grid error when little changes. A balanced endpoint makes changed regions count more. Neither weighting rule is universally correct. Each defines what the experiment values.

Pooling and patient weighting

Pooled

Average all eligible observations together. A person with many visits contributes more weight.

Patient-uniform

Average observations within each patient first, then average patient errors. Each person contributes equal weight.

The chosen averaging target is the estimand. A result can change with the estimand without either calculation being erroneous. A persistence-relative error below one favors the model on its stated scoring rule; above one favors persistence. A patient-uniform average of within-patient ratios need not equal the ratio of two overall MAEs. Do not reconstruct a relative score by dividing adjacent table cells unless the protocol defines it that way.

What uncertainty does and does not say

The question is not just whether one mean is lower. It is whether the comparison supports the particular claim being made.

Paired patients, not millions of independent voxels

A bootstrap estimates sampling uncertainty by repeatedly resampling observations. Because each patient contributes correlated visits and voxels, these studies resample patients rather than pretending every voxel is independent. A paired comparison resamples the same patients for both methods and computes their difference. That retains the within-patient relationship between methods.

A confidence interval is produced by an estimation procedure with a stated coverage target under its assumptions. It is not the probability that the particular computed interval contains the true value. It also does not account for every possible source of uncertainty, such as a mismeasured target or development choices made on the evaluation cohort.

Training seeds are another source of variation

A random seed fixes choices such as initialization and some training order. Training several seeds tests part of that variability. Averaging each patient's errors over fitted seeds and then bootstrapping patients estimates patient uncertainty conditional on those fitted runs. It is not an unrestricted estimate of training-seed uncertainty.

An ensemble averages predictions before scoring them. Averaging errors after scoring each model is different. R35 and R36 report the latter for their seed summaries. Combining these operations under the word average would obscure what was actually tested.

Superiority, noninferiority, and inconclusive results

Superiority means an improvement over the comparator under the stated test. For an error difference, an interval entirely below zero supports that direction. An interval crossing zero leaves superiority unresolved. It does not prove that the methods are equivalent.

Noninferiority asks whether any worsening stays below a specified allowance. The margin must be fixed and justified for that endpoint. A model can pass a noninferiority rule while failing to establish superiority.

Illustrative calculation, not project data

Suppose an error-difference interval is [−0.07, +0.02] and the permitted worsening is +0.05. The interval crosses zero, so superiority is unresolved. Its upper bound is below +0.05, so it passes that noninferiority rule. It does not show that every capability was retained.

An interval entirely above zero can establish worsening while still crossing a larger harm margin. The result then supports “error increased” without settling “the allowed degradation was exceeded.” This distinction will matter for R36's nuisance experiment.

Multiple comparisons and decision rules

If many comparisons are inspected, some favorable intervals can arise by chance. Multiplicity adjustment changes the uncertainty rule to account for a declared family of tests. R36 uses family-three intervals for three planned representation comparisons. Its protocol uses a two-sided error level of 0.05 / 3. R35 also reports family-twelve adjusted intervals for some contrasts. These are different reporting scopes.

Confirmatory comparisons use a fixed hypothesis, endpoint, analysis, and decision rule. Exploratory analyses investigate patterns and generate hypotheses. A descriptive table is valuable, but it should not be mined for a replacement headline after the primary comparison remains unresolved.

Five statuses worth preserving

  • Supported within scope. The stated evidence meets the stated comparison.
  • Negative. The tested method is worse, or a particular claimed advantage is contradicted. The exact statement matters.
  • Inconclusive. The evidence does not resolve the claim.
  • Invalid or withdrawn. The computation does not answer the claimed question, for example because future inputs were supplied.
  • Proposed or unrun. There is no result to interpret yet.

A technically complete run can occupy any of these categories. Artifact verification, independent result review, scientific success, manuscript readiness, and publication acceptance are separate events.

Project inference rules are in R36 decision margins and reporting validation. The illustrative interval above is teaching arithmetic, not a measurement. The +0.05 R36 allowance discussed later is a research decision, not a clinical threshold.

The project map and the denominators

The research question stayed broader than any one model. The experiments changed as checks exposed limits in the original targets, comparisons, and inputs.

The objective is useful MRI representations that preserve anatomical and pathological state and meaningful longitudinal change while separating acquisition effects. JEPA, lesion-focused networks, forecast heads, and whole-brain approaches are candidate methods. None defines success by itself.

Two development cohorts

LUMIERE is the principal development cohort. Its original encoder split used 65 training patients, 13 development patients, and 13 historical final patients. The latter two groups form 26 patients unseen by the original encoder's training. Repeated downstream examination consumed their status as untouched validation.

SAILOR is a controlled-access development-transfer cohort. Its acquisition and automated-mask sources differ from LUMIERE. The later source-only assessments did not fit or select models using SAILOR outcomes, but repeated research use still prevents describing SAILOR as pristine external validation.

The experimental progression

  1. Initial integrity and JEPA training. Establish that weights and training are numerically meaningful, then compare latent predictions with persistence.
  2. Downstream readouts and transfer. Test response labels, volumes, lead-time signals, and cross-cohort behavior. Correct evaluation overlap and stale caches.
  3. RANO-free anatomical forecasting. Use physical compartment measurements and past-only inputs rather than relying on uncertain clinical-label transfer.
  4. Validity repairs and clean rescues. Withdraw future-leaking forecasts, test bounded optimization alternatives, and repair image-mask geometry and time semantics.
  5. R35. Separate current-state estimation from comparison of two observed scans.
  6. R36 Phase 1. Hold architecture and fitting recipes matched while testing a specific change-supervision objective.

“P1” and “P2” are historical program stages. “A” numbers identify experiments or audits. “R” numbers identify research directions and later investigations. Their numbering does not imply one unchanged protocol across the whole project.

Targets and artifacts changed over time

LUMIERE anatomical targets use recovered DeepBraTumIA atlas masks. SAILOR's primary targets use ONCO masks, with CL masks serving a sensitivity comparison. A cache schema defines the fields and meanings in stored features. A cache can be numerically loadable while retaining old label rules, wrong visit membership, or an obsolete coordinate convention.

Provenance records where an artifact came from. A SHA256 hash identifies its exact bytes. A contract fixes the experimental recipe and intended comparisons. Hashes support identity; they do not prove that an image is biologically meaningful or that the contract answers a worthwhile question.

No single leaderboard

Never rank a legacy latent error, a current-schema RANO score, a native log-volume forecast, and an occupancy-change score in one performance table. They have different targets, inputs, cohorts, fitting budgets, and units.

The authoritative map is Methods. Measurements and correction lineage live in Experiments. Current authorization is owned by Research TODO, not an old launch checklist.

The original JEPA result, corrected

The early work established that the system could train. It did not establish a general advantage over persistence. The correction is more informative than the original headline.

A1 to A7. Make the implementation interpretable

A1 tested one training step and the EMA update. A2 showed why matching checkpoint key names is insufficient. The tested community weight port produced nearly identical features for real MRI and noise despite matching all expected parameter keys. The official checkpoint behaved differently. This is evidence about the tested artifact, not a claim that every community port is defective.

A3 compared pooling conventions. A4 made a narrow preprocessing-fidelity comparison. A5 diagnosed collapse during the corrupt-weight period. These are integrity and implementation investigations, not benchmark wins. The correct lesson is to verify behavior before interpreting a falling loss.

With official weights, A6 and A7 produced healthier monitors. The five-epoch pilot still lost the latent persistence comparison. Extending the five-patient pilot to 25 epochs lowered validation loss but did not overcome persistence on average. One held-out patient was particularly difficult. The small pilot does not support a population-wide conclusion about longitudinal MRI.

A8 and A24. Both predictions need the same target space

The full-cohort champion was selected by validation loss. An early report compared JEPA error in the EMA target space with persistence error in an online-projector space. Because the spaces differed, the resulting improvement ratio and patient-win count did not answer the intended question.

The early “2.7-fold improvement” and “82 of 91 patients” claims must not be reused as current evidence. The corrected same-space count was 41 of 91. A correction is not a reason to delete the history; it is a reason to keep the old number out of the conclusion.

Continuation and alternative objectives

A17 investigated why further training moved away from the champion. Accumulating gradients delayed deterioration and sometimes produced transient improvements. Longer runs did not establish a durable continuation benefit. The first momentum comparison was limited by missing optimizer state. The later clean rescue matrix is a separate, completed test.

A33 was a weak short-resume test of bundled objective changes. A34 restarted the bundled recipe properly from scratch. It did not improve locked RANO representation results within their noise and significantly worsened the overall same-space dynamics gate. This is a negative result for that recipe, not a proof that every self-supervised objective fails.

Publication judgment

The publishable lesson is the controlled comparison and its correction, not a rescued universal JEPA success claim. Training health, persistence comparisons, aggregation sensitivity, and validation selection belong together. The original mixed-space ratio belongs only in a clearly labeled methodological history.

See A1 to A24, training gates, and A33/A34. Exact pilot recipes and older uncertainty omissions remain in their linked frozen sources; this handbook does not invent missing confidence intervals.

Readouts, clinical labels, and transfer

A representation can contain readable signal without demonstrating useful forecasting. This phase also shows how cohort overlap, readout choices, and cache defects can change a conclusion.

A9 to A13. Promising readouts were not the final evaluation

A9's early RANO readouts included an attractive single-split score and a five-fold score over all 91 patients. The latter included patients used to train the encoder. It was not an encoder-unseen evaluation. A favorable small split with rare response classes was also unstable evidence.

A10's surprise score and early A12 transfer AUCs were later superseded by corrected metrics, labels, and cache reconstruction. Surprise here means using representation-prediction error as a score for unexpected change. A contemporaneous mismatch score is not automatically an early-warning system. A13 did not establish better incident-progression prediction than persistence at the later visit horizons tested.

A11 used regularized volume readouts. It illustrated both limited recoverable volume signal and the failure of unregularized high-dimensional fitting. Reading current information from features and predicting future volume remained different questions.

A25 introduced patient-clean evaluation on the 26 encoder-unseen patients, using fixed folds and a declared readout. Its historical within-unseen macro-F1 was 0.309 [0.255, 0.358]. That result predates the survival-free current-schema reset. It must not be compared as if it were the same cache and label contract as the later P0 table.

A26 to A37. Controls constrain the narrative

  • Interfaces and temporal heads. A26's ROI pooling, modality concatenation, and volumetry did not improve the recorded locked result. ROI means region of interest. Its legacy geometry predates the affine repair, so it does not falsify correctly aligned lesion methods. A27 exposed substantial sensitivity to readout seed and width on the reused historical final group.
  • Fine-tuning. A28's supervised LoRA comparison did not establish an improvement. A single seed and readout noise limit the scope.
  • Scratch CNNs. A29 to A31 trained convolutional neural networks from scratch; some were under-trained or collapsed toward a dominant class. Their failure is not a fair demonstration of superiority over a faithfully implemented pretrained comparator.
  • Literature and reduced comparators. A32 corrected a published balanced-accuracy figure that had been read as macro-F1. A35's 22-feature radiomics/growth model was a reduced comparator, not a full reproduction of a literature method. Radiomics means engineered quantitative image features.
  • Concepts and transfer mechanisms. A36 found weak recoverable growth/measurement concepts without a locked gain from post-hoc concatenation. A37's displacement and direction diagnostics were explanatory probes, not a deployable calibration result.

A14 to A23. Cross-site failures have several possible causes

A14's transition atlas found different behavior around surgery and response transitions. Small groups and old caches prevent a clean biological mechanism claim. A15's treatment-conditioned and unconditioned fields were approximately tied in their pre-rebuild experiment. That does not establish that treatment is irrelevant or identify a causal treatment effect.

A16 and later analyses found strong site signal and explored feature alignment. CORAL, covariance alignment, transforms feature distributions using covariance information. Partial recovery under such a transform does not isolate covariance as the cause, especially without a mean-only control. A18's raw-versus-processed image comparison changed masking and registration as well as other preprocessing. It cannot isolate one intensity operation.

A19 to A23 tested reprocessing, quality checks, and cache rebuilding. A23 rejected empty or nonfinite phantom modalities and withdrew the unreproducible A21 skull-out readout recovery. Its rebuilt same-space dynamics ratios were 7.11, 10.5, and 8.95 times persistence for the three recorded preprocessing variants. All favored persistence. They are historical cache-specific diagnostics, not current-schema clinical scores.

Publication judgment

These studies support a careful evaluation and failure-analysis narrative. They do not support a state-of-the-art ranking, validated harmonization, reliable early warning, or successful untouched external transfer. A simplified comparator must be named as simplified, and a literature metric must retain its original definition.

See the current-schema scorecard, locked comparator studies, A14 to A24, and the unresolved literature-attribution cautions. No new literature ranking is attempted here.

A40. A future-input defect invalidated the learned forecast

The learned lesion forecaster appeared to improve forecasting across three seeds. The audit then found that its prediction used the later visit's image/mask features and the gap ending at that later visit. Perturbing future images or gaps changed predictions in all 23 tested LUMIERE patients for each of seeds 42, 43, and 44.

Invalid as forecasting evidence

The learned P2.4 in-domain gains, apparent seed stability, SAILOR transfer, and related adaptation forecasts are withdrawn. These are not valid results with merely wide uncertainty. The model was given information that the claimed forecast was supposed to predict.

The old pair builder also skipped visits with missing masks, creating 123 rows instead of the legitimate 117. The corrected history-only rerun scored relative MAE 1.00595 on those 117 rows, with a paired interval crossing zero. It was approximately tied with persistence and preceded the corrected alignment protocol. It is not an aligned, past-only replacement success.

A41. Useful latent diagnostics are still not physical forecasts

The clean gap and velocity heads used a survival-free teacher. A gap-conditioned head receives a visit interval. A velocity field describes a change direction and magnitude in feature space and can be integrated over an interval.

On the reused 13-patient LUMIERE historical test slice, the clean one-step gap head had persistence-relative latent error 0.358. On developmental SAILOR, one-step zero-shot ratios were 7.409 for the champion, 6.056 for the gap head, and 1.051 for velocity. These are latent-feature errors, not the log-volume MAEs above. Velocity did not establish a patient-uniform SAILOR win, and its pooled interval indicated harm.

The target gaps were supplied retrospectively. Longer horizons counted later observed visits rather than fixed day intervals. The champion reused its one-step output beyond the first horizon rather than rolling predictions forward. These facts prevent a prospective fixed-horizon or physical-volume forecasting claim.

See P1 anatomy evidence, A38 to A41, the A40 withdrawal, and the timing and eligibility contract, the frozen P1 fitting protocol, and the P2 execution record. Older frozen arithmetic diagnostics are not invalidated by the learned-forecaster bug, but their separate geometry limitations remain.

Independently resized LUMIERE masks were found to occupy incorrect image locations, including a hemisphere mismatch. What whole-cohort QA established

The October 8, 2026, source-grid audit covered 599 LUMIERE measured visits and 269 SAILOR measured visits. It resolved 3,451 MRI geometry records and checked 2,604 compartment masks and native volumes. LUMIERE compartment source grids differed from the MRI pre-resize grids; SAILOR ONCO masks shared theirs. This establishes the need for physical mask resampling in the former pathway, not perfect longitudinal anatomical correspondence.

The audit did not resample every visit's voxels. Twenty-one reviewed overlays covered nineteen unique visits. A suspect extracranial contour remained in the primary analysis. Corrected geometry does not retrospectively convert old feature caches into aligned artifacts or show that a newly trained forecast would improve.

Target discontinuities are part of the problem

Transitions with exactly one empty endpoint contributed 24.9% of summed absolute log-volume change across all LUMIERE compartment rows, 32.9% in the locked unseen subset, and 43.7% in SAILOR. These are descriptive target-discontinuity fractions, not confirmed disease events or percentages of patients.

ONCO-versus-CL median mask Dice was 0.650 for enhancement and 0.656 for edema. This disagreement between target sources is informative, but neither source is an independently adjudicated truth. The project has no established segmentation-repeatability estimate or biological noise ceiling.

Chronology and artifact semantics

R34 additionally tested equal-gap order ambiguity, the elapsed-time option, future-input gradients, and checkpoint compatibility. It recorded the default JEPA gradient route without silently adding a projector loss. These are structural safeguards. They do not amount to a trained elapsed-time benefit or a causal treatment-effect result.

Publication judgment

The measurement and chronology audit is worth documenting because it changes which experiments can be interpreted. Its value is methodological transparency. The repairs alone are not evidence that the representation is clinically meaningful or that forecasting is now successful.

R35. Better state measurement, not a paired-model victory

R35's strongest positive result is narrower and more useful than a general representation claim. A densely supervised spatial pipeline estimated the automated in-domain volume target better than its matched frozen scalar-volume control.

The design

R35 used the 65 source patients split into 52 fitting and 13 internal-validation patients. It assessed 149 visits from 26 encoder-unseen LUMIERE development patients and 117 eligible observed pairs from 23 of them. SAILOR supplied 269 visits and 242 pairs from 27 development subjects.

The learned spatial encoder received MRI and explicit MRI-sequence availability channels. It kept spatial feature maps and learned from dense three-compartment masks plus native log-volume targets. Masks were supervision, not MRI inference inputs. The fixed comparison used BRAINIAC features without clinical inputs and a fresh scalar-volume-supervised readout.

State and pair arms trained for 20 epochs under seeds 42, 43, and 44. R35 graded validation-selected best checkpoints. Its patient-level errors were averaged over seeds, not prediction-ensembled. The quoted intervals are descriptive paired patient bootstraps, not new success criteria declared after the results.

The corrected twelve-arm study completed 240 epochs in total. Invalid early mixed-precision runs were excluded. It did not supply missing creation-time MRI fingerprints, a new dense behavioral replay, or expert target validity.

R36. Does balancing change improve the encoder?

A nonlinear decoder learned to produce better observed-change outputs under balanced supervision. The primary matched frozen-probe comparison did not establish that the encoder itself improved.

The hypothesis and the controlled comparison

Changed occupancy was sparse in the prepared targets. The audit reported median changed-grid shares of 0.140% for core, 0.059% for enhancement, and 0.437% for edema. These are descriptive target statistics over the 496 usable LUMIERE pairs, not biological-change prevalence. One native enhancing disagreement disappeared completely after resizing. The audit did not quantify all partial boundary loss or prove that unchanged voxels caused R35's limitations.

R36 therefore tested a specific objective change while matching architecture, initialization, exposure schedule, and probe recipe. The shared spatial encoder retained maps at 96, 48, 24, and 12 locations per axis, ending in a 128-channel 12-cubed feature map. The new change decoder received only signed feature differences. It did not receive absolute images, masks, visit IDs, or dates as extra inference inputs. Zero feature difference does not force this nonlinear decoder's output to zero, and reversing the pair does not automatically negate its output. Identity and reversal checks are therefore required companions, not guaranteed architectural properties.

What the losses actually change

The state loss combines mask binary cross-entropy, Dice loss, and absolute normalized native-log-volume error. Binary cross-entropy penalizes incorrect voxel class probabilities; Dice loss penalizes poor mask overlap. The volume-change term is the same in B, C, and D. Only the occupancy-error weighting differs between B and C.

B = state + volume-delta + 0.5 × whole-grid change
C = state + volume-delta + 0.5 × balanced change

Why the decoder finding must stay separate

The nonlinear change head's descriptive balanced occupancy MAE fell from B's 0.483508 to C's 0.270405 and D's 0.274940 on LUMIERE. SAILOR companion values were 0.468779, 0.342619, and 0.340042. The transfer evidence does not rescue an encoder-progression headline.

D's tradeoff includes measured harm

D's trained-perturbation mean excess false-change error fell relative to C by −0.000220 [−0.000416, −0.000083]. Bias sensitivity improved, but noise excess increased by +0.0000301 [+0.0000182, +0.0000436]. Held-out resolution excess remained unresolved at −0.000575 [−0.001358, +0.000106]. D minus C direct-state error increased by +0.047505 [+0.004175, +0.098686] in native log-volume MAE. The interval lies above zero, establishing worsening, but crosses the +0.05 allowance, leaving violation of that particular margin unresolved. The combined nuisance/retention tradeoff is therefore inconclusive, not a successful invariance result.

The affine-intensity perturbation was largely canceled by explicit per-sequence standardization. Its near-zero effect is not learned scanner invariance. The held-out resolution stress can also remove anatomical information. These limitations stay with the locked test rather than being replaced after the results are known.

Review does not enlarge the claim

The artifact audit checked twelve checkpoints, thirty probes, fifteen assessment sets, 1,950 nuisance rows, and thirty saved ridge bundles. Saved-coefficient replay and independently recomputed patient contrasts reproduced the bounded classifications. The result review approved scoped reporting.

Neither review newly replayed every dense output or established segmentation repeatability, biological attribution, or untouched validation. Empty compartments and the suspect extracranial contour remained in the primary analysis. Phase 2, a future-dynamics proposal, remains unauthorized.

Publication judgment

R36 is worth reporting as a matched, unresolved encoder hypothesis with a descriptive decoder finding, a narrow retention result, and a mixed nuisance tradeoff. It is not a positive longitudinal-encoder or forecasting result. A paper should lead with the primary contrast rather than promote the best companion.

What is worth publishing

The strongest current story is a bounded empirical account of automated state estimation and observed-change evaluation. It is not a validated clinical forecast, a new general-purpose MRI representation, or a demonstrated nuisance-invariant disease model.

Editorial judgment, not a new result verdict

The recommendations in this chapter concern how to write honestly from the existing record. Independent reviews approved scoped reporting, not novelty, venue suitability, clinical utility, submission readiness, or acceptance. A manuscript still needs a checked literature comparison, complete reporting, authorship agreement, and appropriate data-use review.

Recommended primary manuscript

Prepare a focused technical report on state estimation versus observed-change representations. Center R35 and R36 because they make the key controls explicit. Use the earlier forecasting history as motivation and a bounded negative context, not as though all runs formed one prospectively designed controlled study.

The question would be whether improved current-state measurement, explicit pair modeling, and change-balanced supervision deliver distinct benefits. The supported answer is nuanced. The tested dense-supervised state pipeline improves an in-domain automated-volume proxy. Dedicated pair modeling has not earned an advantage over independent state subtraction. Balanced supervision produces a descriptive nonlinear-output improvement, while the primary frozen-probe encoder gain remains unresolved. Nuisance and transfer failures limit stronger claims.

A cautious working title is Evaluating state and observed-change representations in longitudinal glioma MRI. This names the investigation rather than announcing an unearned success. It is not a novelty claim or a recommendation for a particular venue.

Alternative papers and why they are weaker now

Possible secondary report

Auditing longitudinal MRI evaluations

A methods or reproducibility account could teach same-space comparisons, patient overlap, target leakage, geometry, and provenance. The corrections are substantive. Its scientific novelty relative to existing evaluation literature has not been established, so a broad methods-paper claim would require a careful literature check.

Not supported now

A clinical forecasting system

Valid tested forecasts did not establish the intended persistence advantage. Learned P2.4 gains were withdrawn. Target reliability and untouched validation remain unresolved. The current record does not support a clinically useful or treatment-planning product claim.

I would not package the entire chronology as a single new-model success paper. It changes tasks, targets, caches, supervision, and evaluation regimes. A shorter, clearly scoped empirical account is more defensible than a broad narrative that assembles attractive numbers from incompatible experiments.

Statements the evidence has not earned

  • “JEPA beats persistence.” Replace it with the exact aggregation-specific corrected observation, or the negative physical forecast result.
  • “The paired encoder captures additional disease dynamics.” The independent-state control was not beaten conclusively, and the task used both observed scans.
  • “Balanced change supervision improves representation quality.” The primary frozen-accessibility contrast remains inconclusive.
  • “The model separates acquisition nuisance from biological change.” Synthetic tests show unresolved tradeoffs and harm; intensity stability largely follows standardization.
  • “Externally validated on SAILOR.” SAILOR is developmental transfer with different target sources and repeated use.
  • “State of the art.” Scratch CNN failures and a reduced radiomics baseline are not faithful matched literature reproductions.
  • “Ready for treatment counterfactuals.” No intervention-effect identification or clinical decision validation has been established.

An example abstract that stays inside the evidence

We evaluate current-state estimation and observed-change representations for longitudinal glioma MRI using automated compartment targets. A densely supervised spatial pipeline reduces in-domain state-measurement error relative to a frozen scalar-volume control, but the comparison does not isolate architecture from supervision. Dedicated paired modeling does not establish an advantage over differences of independent state estimates. Under matched objective comparisons, change-balanced supervision improves descriptive nonlinear decoder outputs without a resolved primary frozen-probe encoder gain. A specified direct-volume retention criterion passes, while nuisance tradeoffs and developmental transfer remain unfavorable or unresolved. These findings support scoped evaluation of automated imaging proxies rather than biological change, clinical forecasting, or untouched external generalization.

This is a writing example, not a submitted abstract or a new scientific approval. A real manuscript must add exact cohort, protocol, numerical, and uncertainty details required by its reporting format.

The factual boundaries come from the R35 review, the R36 review, current evidence and withdrawals, and D58. No systematic novelty or venue review was conducted for this handbook.

What would change the publication judgment?

The next evidence gap is not automatically another training run. Some unresolved claims need better measurement, a different comparison, or genuinely independent validation.

Separate a reportable finding from a stronger paper

It is possible to publish a carefully scoped negative or inconclusive development study without solving every scientific limitation. The manuscript must make those limitations visible and show why the tested question matters. Stronger biological, clinical, architecture-only, or new-site claims require additional evidence specific to those claims.

A predictive distribution assigns probabilities to possible future outcomes rather than providing only one estimate. A proper probability score rewards an honestly specified distribution. Coverage measures how often a prediction interval contains the observed outcome; sharpness describes how narrow those intervals are. These forecast-uncertainty properties are different from confidence intervals on average performance.

Target reliability is the first interpretive bottleneck

Repeatability concerns repeated measurements of the same underlying state. Biological validity concerns whether the target corresponds to the disease process of interest. A noise ceiling would quantify how target error limits achievable performance. None is supplied by a whole-cohort grid audit.

The source audit found one completely disappearing native enhancing disagreement after resizing. It did not establish widespread complete disappearance. Partial boundary-detail loss remains unquantified. A suspect extracranial contour remains in the primary analysis. Filtering it after seeing model outcomes would not produce an unbiased replacement primary set.

A useful reliability proposal would separate segmentation variation, registration error, anatomical mismatch, postoperative transitions, and actual disease change. Any new annotation or adjudication study needs operator approval for its endpoint, sample, cost, access, and privacy. This handbook selects no such study.

Reserve rather than consume the next test

A future independent evaluation should specify its task and information cutoff before model development uses its outcomes. Check patient overlap, label compatibility, acquisition, timing, and data-use terms. A cohort used to train a teacher cannot later be described as untouched external validation of the teacher's downstream representation.

The literature record also contains unresolved attribution and metric differences. Primary protocols should be checked before comparing published numbers. This handbook has not verified every external study or selected a state-of-the-art benchmark.

Phase 2 is not an automatic continuation

The conditional R36 Phase 2 proposal would predict future representations from history with a fixed encoder and matched controls. It remains unauthorized. Synthetic-action representation work is also unrun and unselected. Neither proposal becomes a result, a selected method family, or an approved launch because it appears in a handbook.

The operator gate

New scientific targets, method-family priorities, data acquisition, expert annotation, and acceptance of a proxy as success require an explicit decision. Existing computation and reporting are closed. Do not rerun completed experiments to reconstruct context or to make the documentation look more complete.

How to verify and reuse this account

The handbook is an explanation of the record. Reproducibility requires knowing which source owns a claim and what an audit actually checked.

The source hierarchy

  1. Experiments owns measurements, controls, uncertainty, corrections, and withdrawals.
  2. Methods owns definitions, fixed protocols, evaluation rules, reproduction commands, and historical identifiers.
  3. Notes and insights owns reasoning and distinguishes evidence from interpretation.
  4. Research TODO owns current actions, blockers, and authorization.
  5. R35 closure and R36 closure bind the completed scoped reviews. Read their checked scope and remaining limits rather than treating approval as global validation.
  6. Source coverage maps the historical capture and later updates. Frozen originals can retain obsolete statements and broken embedded links. Their existence does not restore a withdrawn claim.

Reproduction has several levels

Artifact verification can check saved identities, memberships, file hashes, metric arithmetic, and selected replay. Result review inspects whether the interpretation follows from the evidence. Fresh model inference, a new independent replication, and biological validation are additional activities. None should be inferred from a successful hash check.

The R35 audit retained a limitation in the reference cache's historical stability barrier and metadata-level checkpoint identity checks. Earlier MRI caches lack creation-time image-byte fingerprints. Some dense and shuffle outputs were not independently replayed. The R36 audit replayed saved ridge coefficients and patient-level contrasts, not every dense prediction or original MRI tensor. These limits remain even though scoped reporting was approved.

Weights, normalizers, predictions, configs, splits, eligibility rows, and source identities must remain tied together. A new cache cannot inherit validity from a familiar filename. A checkpoint that loads successfully can still have incompatible time semantics or a different input contract.

Privacy and release

The diagrams here are conceptual. They contain no participant scans or individual predictions. SAILOR remains controlled-access. Do not upload its images, private inventories, model inputs, or participant-level outputs to a public viewer or publication supplement. Releasing code or aggregate reporting does not authorize releasing a dataset or trained weights; data-use and disclosure review are separate requirements.

Source-only fitting is a scientific restriction. Local-only handling is a privacy restriction. Passing one does not imply passing the other. The handbook's source comparison and page checks do not replace either.

The shortest honest summary

The project established a narrower automated-state result and a stronger evaluation record. It has not established useful future forecasting, general encoder superiority, successful nuisance separation, biological change measurement, or untouched external generalization.

That summary preserves the positive, negative, inconclusive, and withdrawn outcomes. It is the starting point for a credible manuscript, not a reason to erase the failed hypotheses or to authorize another one.
