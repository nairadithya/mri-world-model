What the glioma MRI research run established

The project began as a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world model for longitudinal MRI. The results changed the question. Training the latent predictor was possible, but useful next-visit forecasts had to beat copying the current measurement, survive patient-held-out evaluation, and transfer without using future information.

LUMIERE is the main development cohort. SAILOR is a second-site transfer check, not a pristine final holdout.

Current conclusion. The original JEPA representation did not improve physical lesion-volume forecasting over persistence. The later lesion-aware model appeared to improve LUMIERE results, but audit A40 found that it used target-visit image features, target masks, and future gaps. Those forecast gains, the three-seed claim, SAILOR forecast claims, and support-calibration forecasts are withdrawn. The cleaned GPU rescue arms also remained worse than the persistence floor. No causal forecasting gain has been established.

R36 is testing change-sensitive MRI features

Execution status. Target QA and two real MRI optimizer checks passed. The private seed42 capacity run is active. Phase 1 remains incomplete. No new representation-quality result or future forecast is established.

R35 found useful supervised per-scan state readouts, but its paired head did not establish an advantage over independent state differences. R36 holds the backbone architecture and initialization fixed and compares whole-grid signed-change supervision with equal weighting of changed and unchanged regions. Matched, source-fitted linear readouts of frozen final encoders will test feature accessibility.

The target audit preserved 599 visits and 496 pairs. Median changed occupancy was 0.140%, 0.059%, and 0.437% of the grid across the three compartments. One native enhancing-compartment disagreement vanished from the resized masks. Sparse change supports testing region balancing. It does not prove why R35 behaved as it did.

A known suspect extracranial contour remains in the primary analysis. Registration quality, automated-mask repeatability, empty-compartment errors, and nuisance sensitivity remain limits. SAILOR stays local and developmental. Independent review is unavailable. Phase 2 requires a separate operator decision after the Phase 1 report.

Read the R36 execution record and locked experiment plan. Implementation is on exp/r36-delta-objective in the isolated R36 worktree. All four objective arms and their controls must be reported before choosing a follow-up.

The story in five decisions

  1. Prove that the model and data path are real. A1 to A7 tested the training loop, preprocessing, pretrained weights, collapse monitors, and persistence. The first pilot's falling loss was invalid because its community checkpoint produced nearly constant MRI features. With the official BrainIAC weights, loss fell and collapse monitors stayed healthy, but the five-patient pilot still lost to persistence.
  2. Separate a learned objective from a useful forecast. Hero Run 4 reached validation loss 0.0081 and held-out test loss 0.0074. An early 91-patient comparison looked like a 2.7× gain, but paired same-space evaluation corrected the headline. The test-only result was favorable under one patient-uniform aggregation, while pooled comparisons and the overall cohort did not establish a general win. Continued training in Run 5 drifted away from the best checkpoint.
  3. Recheck every promising probe. RANO probes, surprise AUC, temporal readouts, and SAILOR adaptation produced leads, not final validation. The initial CV score of 0.334 included patients used to train the encoder. A25 later measured 0.309 on 26 encoder-unseen patients, but readout-seed variation and repeated use of the final split weakened that result. The survival-free current-schema score was 0.2718 pooled and 0.2247 patient-uniform for forecast states. Surprise did not lead incident progression. Empty SAILOR modalities and mixed latent spaces also changed earlier comparisons. The clean-schema JEPA-error AUC was 0.4971, near chance.
  4. Use physical anatomy as the endpoint. P0 fixed the target as enhancing, necrotic/nonenhancing, and edema/FLAIR volumes in mm³, predicted at the next observed visit. P1 found that the frozen JEPA state was worse than persistence on LUMIERE and SAILOR. Structured lesion features and frozen-image/JEPA fusion did not pass the same persistence gate.
  5. Withdraw the apparent learned-lesion win after a cutoff audit. The three-seed P2.4 report showed LUMIERE gains, but the model consumed the target visit. A40 invalidated those scores. A41's gap and velocity heads concern latent reconstruction, not physical lesion-volume forecasts. The subsequent clean-data GPU matrix failed to beat persistence in every arm.

Why the endpoint changed

The August proposal asked whether a BRAINIAC-based JEPA could predict future MRI states and clinical response. Its design used an online image encoder, an exponential moving average (EMA) target, temporal history, and a persistence baseline. Early work discovered a silent checkpoint-port failure, label and survival leakage risks, a non-independent probe CV, and a persistence comparison that mixed latent spaces. These were not cosmetic fixes. They changed which conclusions the data could support.

By September 21 to 22, the team had a cleaner task contract. P0 used explicit patient, visit and pair identities. It removed eventual survival from prospective inputs, repaired ties in the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), recovered LUMIERE masks, and aligned compartment definitions. Incomplete dates supported "next observed visit" as the horizon. They did not support fixed-day predictions or growth rates. Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (RANO) was removed from the primary target because the SAILOR codebook had no independent confirmation.

P1 then tested past-only forecasts of the three physical volumes. It used five patient-separated folds within the 26 LUMIERE patients not used to train the old encoder. Only 23 had eligible consecutive mask-bearing pairs. Current-volume ridge was statistically unresolved against persistence. The JEPA state was decisively worse. SAILOR was an unchanged developmental transfer check, not an untouched external test.

What the clean rescue branch adds

The later audit lives on local branch exp/clean-data-rescues, whose result commit is 067ed6c. It is not part of pushed main at 0bed48b. The branches split at 768cd7e. Main has two later commits, while the clean-rescue branch has six. The branch reopens a bounded set of old optimization and representation hypotheses against a survival-free checkpoint with verified provenance. Its versioned record is info/clean_rescues.md on that branch. The run outputs under outputs/clean-rescues/ are ignored artifacts.

A40 is the decisive update. The learned lesion model used both endpoint observations to form a transition while predicting the target volume. Future information changed predictions for all 23 evaluated LUMIERE patients across all three seeds. Since every reported seed shared this defect, the apparent P2.4 result is not a valid past-only forecast. A40 also found that dropping maskless visits created nonconsecutive bridges. The repaired history-only code has perturbation and gradient checks, but the branch records no retrained causal lesion score.

A41 separates latent reconstruction from physical forecasting. On the historical 13-patient LUMIERE test split, the gap head beat same-space persistence for one-step latent error, but that split is reused development evidence. The velocity head approached persistence on SAILOR without a patient-uniform significant win. Pooled results indicated harm. These numbers do not restore the invalid lesion-volume claim.

What remains open

  • No causal retraining result replaces the withdrawn P2.4 forecast.
  • No new independent external cohort has been evaluated. SAILOR is developmental transfer, and the clean rescue branch keeps its files private.
  • The SAILOR RANO codebook lacks independent confirmation. RANO and treatment-timing claims remain limited.
  • The date metadata supports next-observed-visit ordering, not fixed-day forecasts or biological growth-rate claims.
  • The local clean rescue branch is separate from pushed main. Its tracked note is info/clean_rescues.md at commit 067ed6c. Its training artifacts are ignored.
