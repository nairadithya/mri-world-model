The original JEPA result, corrected

The early work established that the system could train. It did not establish a general advantage over persistence. The correction is more informative than the original headline.

A1 to A7. Make the implementation interpretable

A1 tested one training step and the EMA update. A2 showed why matching checkpoint key names is insufficient. The tested community weight port produced nearly identical features for real MRI and noise despite matching all expected parameter keys. The official checkpoint behaved differently. This is evidence about the tested artifact, not a claim that every community port is defective.

A3 compared pooling conventions. A4 made a narrow preprocessing-fidelity comparison. A5 diagnosed collapse during the corrupt-weight period. These are integrity and implementation investigations, not benchmark wins. The correct lesson is to verify behavior before interpreting a falling loss.

With official weights, A6 and A7 produced healthier monitors. The five-epoch pilot still lost the latent persistence comparison. Extending the five-patient pilot to 25 epochs lowered validation loss but did not overcome persistence on average. One held-out patient was particularly difficult. The small pilot does not support a population-wide conclusion about longitudinal MRI.

A8 and A24. Both predictions need the same target space

The full-cohort champion was selected by validation loss. An early report compared JEPA error in the EMA target space with persistence error in an online-projector space. Because the spaces differed, the resulting improvement ratio and patient-win count did not answer the intended question.

Withdrawn broad headline

The early “2.7-fold improvement” and “82 of 91 patients” claims must not be reused as current evidence. The corrected same-space count was 41 of 91. A correction is not a reason to delete the history; it is a reason to keep the old number out of the conclusion.

Continuation and alternative objectives

A17 investigated why further training moved away from the champion. Accumulating gradients delayed deterioration and sometimes produced transient improvements. Longer runs did not establish a durable continuation benefit. The first momentum comparison was limited by missing optimizer state. The later clean rescue matrix is a separate, completed test.

A33 was a weak short-resume test of bundled objective changes. A34 restarted the bundled recipe properly from scratch. It did not improve locked RANO representation results within their noise and significantly worsened the overall same-space dynamics gate. This is a negative result for that recipe, not a proof that every self-supervised objective fails.

Publication judgment

The publishable lesson is the controlled comparison and its correction, not a rescued universal JEPA success claim. Training health, persistence comparisons, aggregation sensitivity, and validation selection belong together. The original mixed-space ratio belongs only in a clearly labeled methodological history.
