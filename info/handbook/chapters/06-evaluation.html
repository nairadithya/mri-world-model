Who was used to fit what?

A performance number has no clear meaning until we know which patients trained the encoder, selected the model, fitted the readout, and supplied the evaluation observations.

A cohort is a group of participants used in a study. Training data fit parameters. Validation data select settings or checkpoints. Evaluation data measure performance under a declared protocol. A checkpoint is a saved set of model parameters. If evaluation results repeatedly influence choices, that cohort becomes development evidence even if its directory is still called test.

The patient is not the visit

One patient contributes several correlated scans. Randomly splitting visits can place one person's earlier scan in training and later scan in evaluation. For a claim about unseen patients, all visits of a person must stay in the same group. Cross-validation, or CV, rotates which patient groups are held out. It does not create new independent patients.

A nested evaluation uses an inner training-only procedure to select readout settings and an outer procedure to evaluate them. It prevents setting selection from directly using the outer outcomes. It still cannot undo earlier development on the same cohort.

The fitting budget belongs beside the result

A fitting budget includes the number and identity of source patients, labels, training epochs, feature dimensions, tuning choices, and model-selection opportunities. Comparing a densely supervised model with a frozen scalar readout is not an architecture-only comparison. Fitting a readout within the assessment cohort by CV is also not the same protocol as fitting it entirely on separate source patients.

The later studies use the name source52 for a readout trained on 52 source patients. Source26 refers to a separate 26-patient readout budget under its own protocol. Neither should be confused with the 26 encoder-unseen assessment patients. Equal counts do not establish equal participants or equal experimental roles.

Source-only transfer

Transfer evaluates a model or readout on a different cohort or acquisition setting. Zero-shot transfer uses no fitting on target-cohort outcomes. Support-set adaptation fits using a declared subset of target participants and evaluates on different participants. Transductive procedures use information from the evaluation collection itself. These regimes answer different questions.

A normalization step or regularization choice can leak outcome information even if the encoder never changes. A normalizer rescales features or targets using fitted statistics. Source-only normalization estimates those statistics without evaluation outcomes. Selecting the best preprocessing or readout after looking at transfer results also spends evaluation independence.

Two different kinds of generalization

In-domain evaluation assesses held-out patients within the model-development cohort or setting. Cross-domain transfer changes the cohort or acquisition setting. Later in this handbook, the in-domain state result refers to LUMIERE, not SAILOR.

Performance on a new patient from a familiar development setting differs from performance at a genuinely independent site. If a second cohort has been repeatedly examined during development, its results are informative transfer evidence, not untouched external validation. Data access, label definitions, and patient overlap must be checked before stronger claims.

A clean split is necessary, not sufficient

Encoder-unseen means the stated encoder was not trained on those patients. It does not mean the researchers have never examined their outcomes. A renamed test set does not recover independence.
