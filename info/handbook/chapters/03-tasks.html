Three questions that must stay separate

Estimating what is present, measuring a difference we have already observed, and predicting a future observation are different tasks. A successful result on one cannot be relabeled as a success on another.

Let xt denote the MRI at visit t, vt its measured compartment volumes, and Ht all information available through that visit. An index such as t+1 means the next eligible observed visit. It need not represent a fixed number of days.

State and observed change

State estimation predicts a measurement from the current scan. Observed-change assessment uses two scans that are both available. A specialized pair model may compare the scans jointly, but a crucial simple control estimates each state separately and subtracts the estimates.

That subtraction control asks whether the paired architecture adds anything beyond competent single-scan measurement. Beating a predictor of no change is not enough to establish that specialized paired modeling is necessary.

Future prediction and its cutoff

A forecast uses only information available at a specified prediction cutoff. Its inference inputs cannot include the later scan, later mask, later volume, or a gap ending at the later visit when that gap is not known at the cutoff. Future observations can be training targets without being inference inputs. A training target tells the model what it should predict; an input tells it what it is allowed to know. Eventual overall survival records how long a patient lived after a defined starting point. When that later outcome is unavailable at the prediction cutoff, supplying it as an input leaks future information even if it sits in a static clinical-data field. The corrected prospective caches exclude it.

The project generally targets the next observed visit rather than an exact future day. A retrospectively recorded interval describes when the next visit happened. It is not necessarily a planned interval known to a clinician or model beforehand. Giving that interval to a model changes the task to a conditional diagnostic.

Persistence is a meaningful comparator

Persistence predicts that the future measurement equals the current measurement. For a change target, it predicts zero change. This is often difficult to beat when measurements change slowly, future events are uncertain, or measurement noise is substantial. A sophisticated architecture does not get credit merely for being more complicated.

Nuisance and causality

Acquisition nuisance means variation not intended as the disease signal, such as intensity changes caused by an acquisition setting. A synthetic perturbation of one scan can test whether a model invents change when the anatomy was not deliberately altered. But blurring an image can destroy anatomical detail, so not every perturbation cleanly isolates nuisance.

The records sometimes call a future-excluding model causal. Here that means chronological input validity. It does not identify the causal effect of a treatment. Predicting an observed outcome is different from estimating what would have happened under an alternative treatment.

The distinctions are fixed in the assessment protocol, the forecast contract, and Notes and insights.
