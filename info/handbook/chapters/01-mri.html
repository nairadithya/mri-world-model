What an MRI can tell us

An MRI is a measurement of tissue-dependent signal. It is not a photograph of tumor biology. That distinction governs every experiment in this handbook.

A glioma is a tumor arising from the brain's glial lineage. Following a treated glioma requires comparing examinations acquired at different visits. A bright region may reflect tumor, treatment-related change, fluid, or other processes. Image appearance alone does not settle which explanation is correct.

One examination, several kinds of contrast

Magnetic resonance imaging, abbreviated MRI, uses magnetic fields and radiofrequency excitation to measure signal related to tissue properties. A sequence is an acquisition recipe. Different recipes emphasize different properties. In this project, a visit may contain four structural sequences.

From an image to a measurement

A segmentation assigns image locations to labeled regions. A binary mask marks whether a location belongs to a region. The project measures three automated lesion compartments, meaning three labeled anatomical regions.

  • Necrotic/nonenhancing compartment. The target field is necrotic_non_enhancing. Its automated label is not independent confirmation of histological necrosis.
  • Enhancing compartment. The target field is enhancing.
  • Edema/FLAIR compartment. The target field is edema_flair. The name describes an imaging-label category, not a clean biological partition.

These masks are useful proxy targets, measurable stand-ins for a capability of interest. Predicting an automated mask well establishes agreement with that target. It does not, by itself, establish accurate disease measurement. If the automated target is wrong, a model can learn that error faithfully.

Clinical response is a different target

RANO, Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology, is a clinical response framework. The four clean project categories are progressive disease, stable disease, partial response, and complete response, abbreviated PD, SD, PR, and CR. Formal response assessment uses more than a single automated volume change, including clinical and treatment-related context. RANO 2.0 is background reading here, not evidence that this dataset's historical labels were adjudicated under that version.

A surgery-related rating, a missing label, and stable disease are different observations. They must not be merged into one convenient class. Likewise, treatment is an intervention, while a response label describes an outcome. A response label is not a treatment action.

The first distinction

The project asks whether useful information about anatomy and change survives in numerical image features. Automated lesion measurements are one way to test that question. They are neither the entire objective nor biological ground truth.

Project definitions come from Methods, labels and outcomes and the research guardrails. Clinical background is supported by Villanueva-Meyer and colleagues' imaging review and Wen and colleagues' RANO 2.0 criteria, listed in References. This is not a diagnostic handbook.

Before moving on. Be able to distinguish an MRI sequence, an automated mask, a physical volume, and a clinical response label. They describe different levels of evidence.
