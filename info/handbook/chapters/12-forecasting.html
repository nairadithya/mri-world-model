The physical forecast and the leakage withdrawal

The anatomy-first task removed one ambiguity by predicting measured lesion volumes rather than clinical response labels. It did not remove the need for strong baselines or a strict prediction cutoff.

P1. Does history beat the current measurement?

The target is three native compartment log-volumes at the next eligible observed visit. The locked encoder-unseen LUMIERE assessment has 117 rows from 23 patients. Its readouts use five-fold patient CV within the 26 encoder-unseen patients, with ridge strength selected by inner patient-grouped CV. The transfer readout is then fitted on the eligible encoder-unseen LUMIERE patients and applied to 242 SAILOR rows from 27 subjects. This is not R35 or R36's separate source52 fitting budget.

A38 and A39. Structured history and frozen-feature additions

A38 tested a zero-initialized 48-input, 32-hidden-unit, three-output residual MLP using physical measurements. Five locked outer patient folds selected stopping epochs without their test patients. The final source model used all eligible encoder-unseen patients before unchanged SAILOR transfer. A residual model predicts an adjustment to a baseline, rather than rebuilding the whole output. Its output was initialized so that the initial forecast corresponded to persistence. The recorded LUMIERE relative error was 0.997, with paired error-difference interval [−0.096, +0.100]. SAILOR relative error was 1.288 with interval [+0.176, +0.369]. The former was unresolved; the latter was worse.

A39 added fixed vision or JEPA features under the physical-history protocol. Neither established the intended rescue. These are negative or inconclusive comparisons of the stated frozen features and fitting recipe. They do not prove that anatomy is unpredictable or that all image representations are unhelpful.

A40. A future-input defect invalidated the learned forecast

The learned lesion forecaster appeared to improve forecasting across three seeds. The audit then found that its prediction used the later visit's image/mask features and the gap ending at that later visit. Perturbing future images or gaps changed predictions in all 23 tested LUMIERE patients for each of seeds 42, 43, and 44.

Invalid as forecasting evidence

The learned P2.4 in-domain gains, apparent seed stability, SAILOR transfer, and related adaptation forecasts are withdrawn. These are not valid results with merely wide uncertainty. The model was given information that the claimed forecast was supposed to predict.

The old pair builder also skipped visits with missing masks, creating 123 rows instead of the legitimate 117. The corrected history-only rerun scored relative MAE 1.00595 on those 117 rows, with a paired interval crossing zero. It was approximately tied with persistence and preceded the corrected alignment protocol. It is not an aligned, past-only replacement success.

A41. Useful latent diagnostics are still not physical forecasts

The clean gap and velocity heads used a survival-free teacher. A gap-conditioned head receives a visit interval. A velocity field describes a change direction and magnitude in feature space and can be integrated over an interval.

On the reused 13-patient LUMIERE historical test slice, the clean one-step gap head had persistence-relative latent error 0.358. On developmental SAILOR, one-step zero-shot ratios were 7.409 for the champion, 6.056 for the gap head, and 1.051 for velocity. These are latent-feature errors, not the log-volume MAEs above. Velocity did not establish a patient-uniform SAILOR win, and its pooled interval indicated harm.

The target gaps were supplied retrospectively. Longer horizons counted later observed visits rather than fixed day intervals. The champion reused its one-step output beyond the first horizon rather than rolling predictions forward. These facts prevent a prospective fixed-horizon or physical-volume forecasting claim.

See P1 anatomy evidence, A38 to A41, the A40 withdrawal, and the timing and eligibility contract, the frozen P1 fitting protocol, and the P2 execution record. Older frozen arithmetic diagnostics are not invalidated by the learned-forecaster bug, but their separate geometry limitations remain.
