How to verify and reuse this account

The handbook is an explanation of the record. Reproducibility requires knowing which source owns a claim and what an audit actually checked.

The source hierarchy

  1. Experiments owns measurements, controls, uncertainty, corrections, and withdrawals.
  2. Methods owns definitions, fixed protocols, evaluation rules, reproduction commands, and historical identifiers.
  3. Notes and insights owns reasoning and distinguishes evidence from interpretation.
  4. Research TODO owns current actions, blockers, and authorization.
  5. R35 closure and R36 closure bind the completed scoped reviews. Read their checked scope and remaining limits rather than treating approval as global validation.
  6. Source coverage maps the historical capture and later updates. Frozen originals can retain obsolete statements and broken embedded links. Their existence does not restore a withdrawn claim.

Reproduction has several levels

Artifact verification can check saved identities, memberships, file hashes, metric arithmetic, and selected replay. Result review inspects whether the interpretation follows from the evidence. Fresh model inference, a new independent replication, and biological validation are additional activities. None should be inferred from a successful hash check.

The R35 audit retained a limitation in the reference cache's historical stability barrier and metadata-level checkpoint identity checks. Earlier MRI caches lack creation-time image-byte fingerprints. Some dense and shuffle outputs were not independently replayed. The R36 audit replayed saved ridge coefficients and patient-level contrasts, not every dense prediction or original MRI tensor. These limits remain even though scoped reporting was approved.

Weights, normalizers, predictions, configs, splits, eligibility rows, and source identities must remain tied together. A new cache cannot inherit validity from a familiar filename. A checkpoint that loads successfully can still have incompatible time semantics or a different input contract.

Privacy and release

The diagrams here are conceptual. They contain no participant scans or individual predictions. SAILOR remains controlled-access. Do not upload its images, private inventories, model inputs, or participant-level outputs to a public viewer or publication supplement. Releasing code or aggregate reporting does not authorize releasing a dataset or trained weights; data-use and disclosure review are separate requirements.

Source-only fitting is a scientific restriction. Local-only handling is a privacy restriction. Passing one does not imply passing the other. The handbook's source comparison and page checks do not replace either.

Build the reader, not the experiments

python3 -B scripts/build_technical_handbook.py
python3 -B scripts/build_technical_handbook.py --check

These commands regenerate this page and check its source identity, links, and chapter prerequisites. They do not launch research jobs, verify every statement semantically, or perform inference. The maintenance guide describes the authored sources and independent-review requirement. Existing research reproduction commands remain in Methods; they require private artifacts and are not executed by the handbook builder.

Background reading, in teaching order

The following references explain background mechanisms. They are not a systematic literature review or evidence of a matched advantage for this project. No verbatim external passage is reproduced.

  1. Villanueva-Meyer, Mabray, and Cha, 2017. Current Clinical Brain Tumor Imaging. Clinical imaging review for structural MRI sequences and the limits of image-based interpretation. The retrieved bibliographic record identifies these authors; the full PMC page was not extractable during preparation. Specific clinical decisions should be checked in the original review and appropriate current guidance.
  2. NiBabel documentation contributors. Coordinate systems and affines and Working with NIfTI images. Primary software documentation for voxel-to-world mappings and NIfTI header choices. An affine is not proof of accurate registration.
  3. Wen and colleagues, 2023. RANO 2.0: Update to the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology Criteria for High- and Low-Grade Gliomas in Adults. Original consensus criteria. Bibliographic identity and broad clinical-context requirements were checked through indexed records; full-text extraction was unavailable. This reference does not independently validate the project's historical RANO labels or SAILOR mapping.
  4. Assran and colleagues, 2023. Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture. Original I-JEPA paper. It explains prediction in feature space; it is not a longitudinal MRI experiment.
  5. Hu and colleagues, 2021; ICLR 2022. LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models. Original adaptation method. Its language-model evidence does not establish an MRI adaptation gain.
  6. Scikit-learn documentation contributors. Cross-validation iterators for grouped data. Primary implementation documentation for group-aware splitting. The choice of independent unit and intended generalization still belongs to the study design.

The shortest honest summary

The project established a narrower automated-state result and a stronger evaluation record. It has not established useful future forecasting, general encoder superiority, successful nuisance separation, biological change measurement, or untouched external generalization.

That summary preserves the positive, negative, inconclusive, and withdrawn outcomes. It is the starting point for a credible manuscript, not a reason to erase the failed hypotheses or to authorize another one.
