R36. Does balancing change improve the encoder?

A nonlinear decoder learned to produce better observed-change outputs under balanced supervision. The primary matched frozen-probe comparison did not establish that the encoder itself improved.

The hypothesis and the controlled comparison

Changed occupancy was sparse in the prepared targets. The audit reported median changed-grid shares of 0.140% for core, 0.059% for enhancement, and 0.437% for edema. These are descriptive target statistics over the 496 usable LUMIERE pairs, not biological-change prevalence. One native enhancing disagreement disappeared completely after resizing. The audit did not quantify all partial boundary loss or prove that unchanged voxels caused R35's limitations.

R36 therefore tested a specific objective change while matching architecture, initialization, exposure schedule, and probe recipe. The shared spatial encoder retained maps at 96, 48, 24, and 12 locations per axis, ending in a 128-channel 12-cubed feature map. The new change decoder received only signed feature differences. It did not receive absolute images, masks, visit IDs, or dates as extra inference inputs. Zero feature difference does not force this nonlinear decoder's output to zero, and reversing the pair does not automatically negate its output. Identity and reversal checks are therefore required companions, not guaranteed architectural properties.

Original R35 is the initialization reference, not another newly selected model. Each of the four arms starts from its seed-specific R35 state checkpoint and runs exactly 20 epochs. Unlike R35's selected-best recipe, R36 grades the final epoch. All use source52 fitting, the same source-only normalization, three training seeds, and the same ordered real pairs.

What the losses actually change

The state loss combines mask binary cross-entropy, Dice loss, and absolute normalized native-log-volume error. Binary cross-entropy penalizes incorrect voxel class probabilities; Dice loss penalizes poor mask overlap. The volume-change term is the same in B, C, and D. Only the occupancy-error weighting differs between B and C.

B = state + volume-delta + 0.5 × whole-grid change
C = state + volume-delta + 0.5 × balanced change
D = C + 0.25 × nuisance constraintThese are the locked research loss coefficients. They are not clinical constants. A retains the state term alone.

The frozen probe applies local linear readouts to signed multiscale feature differences, resizes their outputs to the prepared grid, and averages across levels. Its source52 recipe and 20-epoch final scoring are fixed. A separate nonlinear decoder is reported as a companion, not substituted for this primary representation test.

Completed budget and uncertainty

All twelve training runs and thirty source-only probes completed. Each training arm had 5,940 real-pair exposures, with 5,928 to 5,936 successful updates after disclosed AMP skips. Assessment retained 149 LUMIERE visits from 26 development patients, 117 pairs from 23 of them, and 269 SAILOR visits and 242 pairs from 27 subjects. No SAILOR fitting or checkpoint selection occurred.

Why the decoder finding must stay separate

The nonlinear change head's descriptive balanced occupancy MAE fell from B's 0.483508 to C's 0.270405 and D's 0.274940 on LUMIERE. SAILOR companion values were 0.468779, 0.342619, and 0.340042. These are three-seed mean patient errors under the same source52 study, not fresh inferential success tests.

They show that the trained output pipeline responds to the weighting. They do not establish the primary frozen-encoder gain. On SAILOR, every frozen difference probe had higher descriptive error than its independent-state control. Every source52 ridge volume-change mean also exceeded zero change. The transfer evidence does not rescue an encoder-progression headline.

D's tradeoff includes measured harm

D's trained-perturbation mean excess false-change error fell relative to C by −0.000220 [−0.000416, −0.000083]. Bias sensitivity improved, but noise excess increased by +0.0000301 [+0.0000182, +0.0000436]. Held-out resolution excess remained unresolved at −0.000575 [−0.001358, +0.000106]. These are local occupancy false-change endpoints, not native-volume units.

D minus C direct-state error increased by +0.047505 [+0.004175, +0.098686] in native log-volume MAE. The interval lies above zero, establishing worsening, but crosses the +0.05 allowance, leaving violation of that particular margin unresolved. The combined nuisance/retention tradeoff is therefore inconclusive, not a successful invariance result.

The affine-intensity perturbation was largely canceled by explicit per-sequence standardization. Its near-zero effect is not learned scanner invariance. The held-out resolution stress can also remove anatomical information. These limitations stay with the locked test rather than being replaced after the results are known.

Review does not enlarge the claim

The artifact audit checked twelve checkpoints, thirty probes, fifteen assessment sets, 1,950 nuisance rows, and thirty saved ridge bundles. Saved-coefficient replay and independently recomputed patient contrasts reproduced the bounded classifications. The result review approved scoped reporting.

Neither review newly replayed every dense output or established segmentation repeatability, biological attribution, or untouched validation. Empty compartments and the suspect extracranial contour remained in the primary analysis. Phase 2, a future-dynamics proposal, remains unauthorized.

Publication judgment

R36 is worth reporting as a matched, unresolved encoder hypothesis with a descriptive decoder finding, a narrow retention result, and a mixed nuisance tradeoff. It is not a positive longitudinal-encoder or forecasting result. A paper should lead with the primary contrast rather than promote the best companion.
