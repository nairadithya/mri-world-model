Clean rescues and the measurement repairs

Two kinds of work followed the audits. One tested whether bounded training changes rescued the forecast. The other repaired what the images, masks, timestamps, and artifacts meant.

R33. Seven completed GPU rescue arms

The clean rescue matrix retained the official initialization, original 65/13/13 patient identities, seed 42, survival-free inputs, locked unseen folds, and unchanged evaluation rows. The four optimizer/accumulation arms independently resumed the same control for six epochs. Augmentation, surgery filtering, and transition weighting were separate 30-epoch scratch runs. They were not selected additions to one growing recipe.

The completed table below reports pooled persistence-relative anatomy errors. The original protocol also called for patient-uniform uncertainty, but these particular values do not constitute new patient-uniform bootstrap verdicts. All arms remained above persistence on the reported endpoint.

Loaded momentum did not rescue the anatomy result. Training around surgery or emphasizing transitions also did not reach the reported floor. These negative comparisons justify closing the bounded matrix. They do not justify endlessly reopening the same arms or declaring that all longitudinal approaches fail.

R34. A coordinate defect can survive plausible arithmetic

Independently resized LUMIERE masks were found to occupy incorrect image locations, including a hemisphere mismatch. Native volumes could still agree because moving a mask does not necessarily change its voxel count. The repair used verified MRI physical grids and content-bound geometry provenance rather than interpreting the resized identity affine as a physical map.

Three additional sampled LUMIERE visits had legacy-versus-aligned union Dice of 0.0000, 0.0181, and 0.0291. Three sampled SAILOR visits had Dice 1.0000. These six comparisons diagnose the old coordinate pathway. They are not an estimate of segmentation accuracy or a population-wide error rate.

What whole-cohort QA established

The October 8, 2026, source-grid audit covered 599 LUMIERE measured visits and 269 SAILOR measured visits. It resolved 3,451 MRI geometry records and checked 2,604 compartment masks and native volumes. LUMIERE compartment source grids differed from the MRI pre-resize grids; SAILOR ONCO masks shared theirs. This establishes the need for physical mask resampling in the former pathway, not perfect longitudinal anatomical correspondence.

The audit did not resample every visit's voxels. Twenty-one reviewed overlays covered nineteen unique visits. A suspect extracranial contour remained in the primary analysis. Corrected geometry does not retrospectively convert old feature caches into aligned artifacts or show that a newly trained forecast would improve.

Target discontinuities are part of the problem

Transitions with exactly one empty endpoint contributed 24.9% of summed absolute log-volume change across all LUMIERE compartment rows, 32.9% in the locked unseen subset, and 43.7% in SAILOR. These are descriptive target-discontinuity fractions, not confirmed disease events or percentages of patients.

ONCO-versus-CL median mask Dice was 0.650 for enhancement and 0.656 for edema. This disagreement between target sources is informative, but neither source is an independently adjudicated truth. The project has no established segmentation-repeatability estimate or biological noise ceiling.

Chronology and artifact semantics

R34 additionally tested equal-gap order ambiguity, the elapsed-time option, future-input gradients, and checkpoint compatibility. It recorded the default JEPA gradient route without silently adding a projector loss. These are structural safeguards. They do not amount to a trained elapsed-time benefit or a causal treatment-effect result.

Publication judgment

The measurement and chronology audit is worth documenting because it changes which experiments can be interpreted. Its value is methodological transparency. The repairs alone are not evidence that the representation is clinically meaningful or that forecasting is now successful.
